Unvented Cylinders Explained

What an Unvented Cylinder Is and Why It Matters

An unvented hot water cylinder is a sealed, pressurised cylinder fed directly from the mains cold water supply. Because it takes its pressure straight from the mains rather than from gravity, it delivers strong, consistent hot water to every outlet in the property — bathroom, en-suite, kitchen — at the same time, without the flow dropping off when more than one tap or shower is running.

The traditional alternative is a vented, gravity-fed system: a cold water storage tank in the loft feeding a cylinder by gravity. That setup depends entirely on the height of the tank above the outlet, which is why gravity-fed showers in older Wirral properties are often weak, and why a loft conversion or extra bathroom can be difficult to plumb without upgrading the system.

Fitting an unvented cylinder removes the cold water storage tank from the loft altogether, freeing up loft space, and gives every outlet in the house mains pressure. We supply and install Megaflo and equivalent unvented cylinders, sized correctly for the property and the household's hot water demand.

A Legal Requirement

Why G3 Qualification Is a Legal Requirement

Unvented hot water systems are pressurised, which means they carry a risk of failure if they're installed, commissioned or serviced incorrectly. For that reason, the installation and annual servicing of an unvented cylinder must be carried out by an engineer holding a valid G3 qualification, under Building Regulations Approved Document G3.

We are G3 qualified. That covers correct sizing of the cylinder and expansion vessel, fitting and testing of the safety devices — including the temperature and pressure relief valve, expansion relief valve and non-return valves — and running the pressure and temperature checks needed to commission the system safely before it's put into use.

If you're getting quotes for an unvented cylinder, it's worth asking directly whether the engineer carrying out the work holds a G3 qualification. Not every general plumber does, and an incorrectly installed unvented system is a genuine safety risk, not just a compliance box to tick.

Larger Wirral Properties

Sizing for Larger Wirral Homes

We fit a lot of unvented cylinders in the larger detached and period properties around Heswall, Caldy and West Kirby, and the reason is usually the same: two or more bathrooms and a mains pressure that a combi boiler simply can't cope with.

A combi boiler heats water on demand and pushes it out at whatever the incoming mains pressure allows — it has no stored reserve to draw on. That's fine for a single bathroom, but ask a combi to supply a main bathroom shower and an en-suite at the same time and the flow rate to both will drop noticeably. In a larger home with multiple bathrooms, that's a daily frustration, not an occasional inconvenience.

An unvented cylinder holds a reserve of hot water at mains pressure, so two bathrooms running simultaneously each get a strong, steady flow rather than sharing whatever the boiler can produce in the moment. For bigger Wirral homes with several bathrooms, this is usually a better fit than a combi, and it's the main reason we get called out to these properties specifically for cylinder installation rather than a straight boiler swap.

Correct sizing matters here — a cylinder that's too small for the household will run out of hot water at peak times, and one that's oversized wastes energy reheating water that isn't used. We size the cylinder to the number of bathrooms, the boiler's output, and how the household actually uses hot water, rather than fitting a standard size regardless of the property.

Related Service

Boosted Cold Water Storage for Low-Pressure Properties

An unvented cylinder needs adequate mains pressure and flow to work properly. Most Wirral properties have enough, but some — particularly at the end of a long supply run, on higher ground, or in older streets with narrower mains — genuinely don't have the pressure an unvented system needs.

For those properties, we also install boosted cold water storage systems: a break tank and pump set that takes in mains water at low pressure, stores it, and boosts it back out at a pressure suitable for an unvented cylinder and the rest of the property's plumbing. This is a different problem from the multiple-bathroom flow issue described above — it's specifically for properties where the incoming mains pressure itself is too low, not just where demand from several outlets outstrips a combi.

If you're not sure whether your mains pressure is adequate, we can check this as part of a site visit before recommending an unvented cylinder, a boosted system, or both.

Ongoing Compliance

The Annual Service Obligation

Unvented cylinders legally require an annual service and safety check, carried out by a G3-qualified engineer. This isn't optional maintenance — it's a condition of the system continuing to operate safely, and it's usually a requirement of the manufacturer's warranty too.

The annual service checks that the safety devices — the temperature and pressure relief valve, expansion vessel, and non-return valves — are all functioning correctly, and that the system pressure and temperature are within safe limits. We carry out annual servicing on cylinders we've installed and on existing unvented systems fitted by others.

Building Regulations

Discharge Pipework and Building Regulations Sign-Off

Every unvented cylinder needs discharge pipework — the D1 and D2 pipework that safely carries away any water released by the temperature and pressure relief valve or expansion relief valve in the unlikely event they operate. This pipework has to be routed and terminated correctly: a visible, safe discharge point where any release would be noticed, and pipe runs, falls and lengths that comply with the Building Regulations guidance for unvented systems.

Because unvented cylinder installation falls under Building Regulations, the work needs to be notified and signed off — either through a local authority Building Control application, or via a competent person scheme. As part of our installation service we handle the discharge pipework design and the Building Regulations notification, so you're not left chasing paperwork after the job.

Common Questions

Unvented Cylinder FAQs

What's the difference between vented and unvented?

A vented system uses a cold water storage tank in the loft to feed the cylinder by gravity, so pressure depends on the height of the tank above the outlet. An unvented system is sealed and fed directly from the mains, so it delivers mains pressure to every outlet — including multiple bathrooms at once — without needing a loft tank at all.

Do I need a service every year?

Yes. Unvented cylinders are pressurised systems and legally require an annual service and safety check by a G3-qualified engineer, which is also usually a condition of the manufacturer's warranty. We carry out annual servicing on cylinders we've installed and on existing systems fitted by others.

Can any plumber fit an unvented cylinder?

No. Installation and servicing of unvented hot water systems must be carried out by an engineer holding a valid G3 qualification, under Building Regulations Approved Document G3. Not every general plumber holds this qualification, so it's worth checking directly before booking any unvented cylinder work.

Will it fit where my old tank was?

Usually, yes — an unvented cylinder typically replaces an existing vented cylinder in the same airing cupboard or utility space, and the loft storage tank is removed rather than replaced. We assess the existing pipework, space and discharge route on a site visit to confirm exactly what's needed before quoting.

What size cylinder do I need?

It depends on the number of bathrooms, household size and hot water usage patterns, as well as the boiler's output. Larger homes with two or more bathrooms — common in Heswall, Caldy and West Kirby — generally need a larger cylinder than a single-bathroom property. We size the cylinder correctly as part of the quote, rather than fitting a standard size regardless of the property.
