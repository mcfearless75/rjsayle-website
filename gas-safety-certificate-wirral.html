Landlord Obligations

What the law requires

Under the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998, landlords in England and Wales have a legal duty to have every gas appliance, flue and piece of associated pipework in a rented property checked annually by a Gas Safe registered engineer. This applies whether the property is a single buy-to-let or one flat within a larger portfolio.

The check needs to be renewed within a window around the anniversary of the last one — the widely used approach is to carry out the new check anywhere from about 10 to 12 months after the previous check, and no later than 2 months (roughly 28 days) before the existing certificate expires, so the original renewal date is preserved rather than drifting later each year. If you're unsure exactly where your property sits in that window, it's worth confirming the precise dates with us or checking your existing certificate before booking, since getting this wrong can mean losing your anniversary date or leaving a property briefly without valid cover.

Once the check is complete, you must give your tenant a copy of the current gas safety record within 28 days of the check, or hand a copy to any new tenant before they move in. Records — including previous years' certificates — should be kept for at least 2 years, and we'd suggest keeping them for longer if you can, particularly across a portfolio where inspection history helps spot recurring issues.

The Inspection

What we check

A CP12 isn't a quick glance at the boiler. Every gas appliance the landlord is responsible for gets a full safety inspection.

Gas appliances

Boilers, gas fires, gas cookers and hobs supplied by the landlord — checked for safe operation and correct installation.

Pipework and connections

A tightness test on the gas installation pipework to check for leaks, plus a visual check of accessible pipework condition.

Flues and ventilation

Flue performance and safe removal of combustion products, plus adequate ventilation to each appliance.

Safety devices

Flame failure devices and other safety cut-outs tested to confirm they shut the appliance down as designed.

Operating pressure & burner check

Burner pressure or heat input checked against the manufacturer's data plate to confirm the appliance is running correctly.

Visual condition

Overall condition and location of each appliance assessed, including whether it's suitable for the room it's fitted in.

If we find anything that needs remedial work during the inspection, we'll explain it clearly, quote for the fix, and — where it's safe to do so — get it sorted on the same visit.

Local Knowledge

Neston and Ellesmere Port landlords: a different council, a genuine difference

Most of the areas we cover — Birkenhead, Wallasey, Bebington, Heswall, West Kirby, Hoylake — sit under Wirral Council. Neston and Ellesmere Port don't. Both fall under Cheshire West and Chester Council, a separate local authority with its own planning, licensing and enforcement teams.

For a landlord, that matters in practical terms: any selective licensing schemes, HMO licensing conditions, or local enforcement priorities that apply to a property in Neston or Ellesmere Port are set by Cheshire West and Chester, not Wirral Council, and can differ from what a landlord with a Birkenhead or Wallasey property is used to. It's a detail that's easy to miss if you're managing properties across both areas and assuming the same rules apply everywhere.

We work across both authorities, so whether your property sits in Wirral Council's area or Cheshire West and Chester's, we know which council you're dealing with and can flag it if it's relevant to a job.

Looking Ahead

Beyond CP12: the EPC C deadline of 1 October 2030

A CP12 is an annual, recurring obligation. Alongside it, there's a longer-term change landlords need to have on their radar: from 1 October 2030, privately rented properties in England and Wales will need to meet a minimum EPC rating of C.

Dual metric explained

The proposed standard uses two metrics: a fabric performance metric, which is the primary requirement and covers things like insulation and the building fabric, plus a secondary metric — either smart readiness or the heating system — which the landlord can choose between. A heating system upgrade, such as a new efficient boiler, is one route to meeting that secondary element.

£10,000 cost cap

There's a proposed £10,000 cost cap per property for the works needed to reach EPC C. Costs incurred from 1 October 2025 onwards are expected to count towards that cap, so it's worth keeping records of any relevant work carried out from that date.

Penalties up to £30,000

Non-compliance is expected to carry penalties of up to £30,000 per property, which makes this a significantly bigger financial exposure than a missed or late CP12 renewal, and worth planning for well ahead of the 2030 deadline rather than leaving it to the last year.

Where a heating upgrade fits the secondary metric

If you're planning boiler or heating system work on a rental property anyway — replacing an ageing boiler, for example — it's worth thinking about how that work fits into your route to EPC C, since an efficient heating system can help meet the secondary metric alongside fabric improvements. We install boilers and heating systems, and can talk through how a planned upgrade sits within your broader EPC C timeline, but the fabric side of a property (insulation, glazing and similar) sits outside what we do and would need a separate assessment.

Common Questions

Gas Safety Certificate (CP12) FAQs

How often does a rental property need a gas safety check?

At least once every 12 months. To keep your renewal date from drifting, the usual approach is to book the next check any time from around 10 to 12 months after the last one, and no later than about 2 months before the current certificate expires. If you're not sure exactly where your property sits in that window, check your existing certificate or ask us and we'll confirm the right date.

What happens if my CP12 has expired?

You're operating outside your legal obligation as a landlord, which puts you at risk if anything goes wrong and can cause problems at the point of sale, remortgage, or if a council or tenant asks to see a current certificate. The fix is straightforward — book a new check as soon as possible. Call us and we'll prioritise getting an engineer out.

Can I get the certificate the same day?

Yes. Once the inspection is complete and everything passes, we issue your CP12 as a digital copy on the same visit, ready to forward to your tenant or letting agent straight away.

Do I need a CP12 if the property is all-electric?

No — the CP12 requirement applies specifically to gas appliances, so if there are none in the property, there's nothing for a gas safety record to cover. Bear in mind that electrical safety is a separate legal duty (an EICR), which isn't something we carry out — you'd need a qualified electrician for that.

Will a new boiler help me hit EPC C by 2030?

It can help. The proposed EPC C standard uses a fabric metric as the primary requirement, plus a secondary metric that's either smart readiness or the heating system — a landlord's choice. An efficient new boiler is one way of meeting that secondary heating-system element. It won't address the fabric side of a property on its own, so it's worth thinking of it as part of a wider plan rather than a complete fix.
