Liverpool's Water Hardness Varies by Postcode

Liverpool doesn't have one water hardness — it has two distinct zones, and it matters for anyone thinking about limescale, boiler efficiency or scale-related breakdowns. In the southern L10–L19 postcodes, water runs at roughly 122–131ppm, which puts it in the moderately hard category. In a boiler, unvented cylinder or shower system, that's enough hardness over time to build scale on heat exchangers and reduce efficiency, so a scale reducer or periodic power flush is genuinely worth considering in these areas.

Move into the central and northern L20–L29 postcodes and the water is much softer, typically around 34–43ppm. At that level, limescale isn't really a meaningful concern for heating systems. This is general regional water-hardness information rather than a reading for any specific property — supply can vary street to street — but it's a useful starting point if you're weighing up whether a scale protection device is worth adding to a new boiler installation.

City-Centre Apartments and Georgian Conversions

A lot of our Liverpool enquiries come from a property type we see far less of on the Wirral: multi-storey apartment blocks and Georgian townhouses split into flats. These jobs are genuinely different from a standalone house. Many blocks run on communal or shared heating systems rather than individual boilers, and where flues do serve individual flats, they often route through communal risers or shared external walls across several floors — which changes what's technically possible and what building regulations require.

Before any work starts on a flue, heating system or gas appliance in one of these buildings, we need written consent from the freeholder or managing agent. That's not optional paperwork — altering shared building fabric or communal plant without permission can breach the lease and create real problems for the leaseholder. It also affects access: getting into plant rooms, risers or communal areas often means coordinating with a managing agent or concierge rather than just turning up with a key.

If you're in one of these buildings, factor in extra lead time for permissions before booking a firm date. We're happy to advise on what consent you'll need and help you approach the right person, but it's genuinely a different job to a Wirral semi, and we'd rather you knew that upfront.
