Single Radiator Work

Replacing a Single Radiator

Most radiator call-outs are a single unit that's failed — corroded through, leaking at a valve joint, or simply cold at the top no matter how long the heating's been on. Where it's a like-for-like swap, we isolate the radiator at its own valves, drain that radiator only, and fit the replacement without disturbing the rest of the system. In most cases this is a same-day job.

If the room has always struggled to warm up, replacing like-for-like just repeats the same problem with a new radiator. We'll often recommend upgrading to a larger output radiator on the existing pipework — the connections stay in the same place, but the radiator itself is sized correctly for the room.

Where a radiator needs to move — under a different window, out from behind a new piece of furniture, or as part of a room reconfiguration — we extend or reroute the pipework and cap off the old run. Depending on the property this can mean lifting a floorboard or chasing a short run into a wall, and we'll always explain what that involves before we start.

Full System Upgrades

New Circuits, Extra Radiators and Zoning

Extensions, loft conversions and knocked-through rooms usually mean the existing central heating circuit needs extending — new pipework run to additional radiators, and the boiler and pump checked to make sure they can cope with the extra load. We plan the pipe routes to minimise disruption and keep the finished job tidy.

For larger properties, or homes with very different heating needs upstairs and downstairs, we can fit zone valves and separate thermostats so different parts of the house heat independently. This is particularly useful where an extension or converted loft is used less often than the rest of the house — no point heating a spare room to the same schedule as the kitchen.

Where an existing system is old, poorly designed, or has been extended piecemeal over the years by different tradespeople, a full central heating installation can be more sensible than patching it further. See our boiler installation page for how this fits together with a new boiler.

Getting It Right First Time

Sizing Radiators for Wirral's Older Housing Stock

This is where a lot of radiator installs go wrong, and it's worth explaining properly. Radiator sizing calculators and generic heat loss tables are usually built around cavity wall construction with modern insulation. Plug in a room's dimensions without adjusting for wall type and you'll get a radiator that's the right size for a 1990s cavity-wall semi — not for the house it's actually going in.

Wirral has a lot of solid-wall Victorian and Edwardian terraces and villas — West Kirby, Birkenhead and parts of Wallasey all have plenty of them. Solid brick walls lose heat far faster than a cavity wall, insulated or not. A radiator sized on cavity-wall assumptions will be undersized for a solid-wall room of the same dimensions, and the result is a radiator that gets hot but never quite heats the room — especially on colder days when it matters most.

Before we spec a radiator we work out the room's actual heat loss — wall construction, window size and glazing type, room dimensions and how exposed the walls are — rather than reading a number off a generic chart. It takes a bit longer up front, but it means the radiator you get actually does the job.

System Efficiency

Balancing and TRVs

Balancing a central heating system means adjusting the lockshield valve on each radiator so hot water flows through the whole circuit evenly, rather than taking the path of least resistance through the radiators closest to the boiler. An unbalanced system typically means radiators near the boiler get too hot too fast, while radiators at the far end of the circuit — often upstairs, or in an extension — stay lukewarm however long the heating runs.

The knock-on effect is wasted money: the boiler has to run longer to try to heat the cold rooms, cycling on and off inefficiently, while the near radiators are already overheating and being turned down or ignored. A properly balanced system heats every room at roughly the same rate and lets the boiler work as intended.

TRVs — thermostatic radiator valves — sit alongside balancing rather than replacing it. They let you set an individual target temperature per room, so a bedroom that's used less can run cooler than a living room. We fit TRVs to every radiator except the one in the room with your main room thermostat, which needs a manual valve so the two controls aren't working against each other. See power flushing if sludge and scale build-up is also affecting circulation.

Period Properties

Column and Period Radiators in Conservation Areas

Wirral has 26 conservation areas, including Port Sunlight and Hamilton Square, where external appearance can be a factor in what's acceptable — this mainly affects visible pipework on the outside of a building rather than what's fitted inside. Inside a period property, though, some homeowners prefer column or other period-style radiators to suit the character of the room rather than a standard modern panel radiator. We can fit these where they're a good match for the room, and we'll flag it if a particular style or position needs a bit more thought given the property.

Pricing

What It Costs

Cost depends on the scope of the job — a like-for-like radiator swap is a very different job from a full central heating circuit for an extension, and pricing reflects that. Factors that affect the price include whether a radiator is being upgraded in size, whether pipework needs to move, how accessible the property is, and whether the job is a single radiator or part of a larger system upgrade.

Common Questions

Radiators & Central Heating FAQs

How do I know if my radiator is too small for the room?

The room never quite gets warm even with the radiator on full, the boiler runs constantly trying to satisfy the thermostat, or the radiator is barely warm at the top while hot at the bottom. Undersized radiators are common in older Wirral properties where the original heating was designed for a different insulation standard than the house actually has. We measure the room and calculate the correct output rather than guessing.

Can you fit radiators without draining the whole system?

For a single like-for-like radiator swap, we can usually isolate just that radiator using its own valves and lockshield, so the rest of the system stays full and pressurised. A full drain-down is only needed for bigger jobs — new pipework circuits, moving a radiator to a different wall, or working on a system with valves that won't isolate cleanly.

What's a TRV and do I need one on every radiator?

A TRV (thermostatic radiator valve) lets you set a target temperature for an individual room rather than relying on one thermostat for the whole house. We fit them on every radiator except the room where your main room thermostat is located — that one needs a manual valve, otherwise the TRV and the room stat can end up fighting each other.

How long does a single radiator replacement take?

A straightforward like-for-like swap is usually a same-day job. If we're upgrading to a larger radiator on the existing pipework, it's still normally completed in a day. Moving a radiator to a new position, or work that needs floorboards up or pipework extended, can take longer — we'll give you a clear timeframe before we start.

Do you fit designer and vertical radiators?

Yes. Vertical, column and other designer radiators can usually be plumbed into an existing central heating circuit, though the pipework connections sometimes need adapting depending on the model and where it's going. Bring us the make and model you're considering and we'll confirm it's compatible with your system before you buy.
