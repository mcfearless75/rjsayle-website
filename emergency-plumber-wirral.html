Know Before You Call

What Counts as an Emergency

Not every plumbing problem needs a same-day call-out, and being honest about that saves you money. The situations below are genuine emergencies — call us straight away for any of these:

Burst pipes — water you can't stop or contain, especially near electrics.

Major leaks — water coming through ceilings, walls, or pooling faster than you can mop it up.

Total loss of heating or hot water — particularly in cold weather, or where there are elderly residents, young children, or anyone vulnerable in the property.

Boiler breakdown in cold weather — no heating overnight in freezing temperatures is a genuine risk, not just an inconvenience.

Uncontrollable leaks — a leak you can't stop by isolating a valve or turning off the stopcock, particularly from a cylinder, tank, or a pipe behind a wall.

A dripping tap, a slow-draining sink, or a boiler that's simply due a service aren't emergencies — those are better booked in as a normal appointment via our contact page, and it'll cost you less.

While You're Waiting

Do This Right Now While You Wait

A few minutes of the right action can limit the damage before we arrive.

Find Your Stopcock

This is the single most useful thing you can do in a burst pipe or major leak. Turn it clockwise to shut off the mains water supply to the whole property. In most Wirral homes it's under the kitchen sink; in some it's near the front of the property, in a cupboard, or under the stairs. Find it now, before you need it in a hurry.

Isolate the Boiler

If the leak or fault is coming from the boiler itself, switch it off at the boiler and at the wall or fused spur if it's safe to reach. Don't attempt to open the casing or touch internal components — leave that to us.

Contain the Leak

Once the water's isolated, mop up or bucket what you can, move electrical items and valuables away from the water, and if water is coming through a ceiling, pierce it carefully with something like a screwdriver over a bucket to relieve the pressure and stop it spreading further across the ceiling void.

When to Call the Gas Emergency Line — 0800 111 999

If you smell gas, or a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, this is not something to call us about first. Leave the property immediately, don't operate light switches or use any naked flames, and call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 straight away. That's a genuine 24-hour emergency line — we are not, and we'd rather you called the people equipped to make the situation safe.

No Surprises

What an Emergency Call-Out Involves

We won't quote you a fixed call-out fee on the phone, and we're not going to pretend that's not a little frustrating — but it's more honest than making up a number. Emergency work varies enormously depending on what's actually wrong, what parts are needed, how accessible the fault is, and what time of day it is. A quick isolation and repair is a very different job to tracing a hidden leak behind a wall at 9pm.

Here's how it works in practice: you call and describe the problem, we ask enough questions to understand the scale of it, and we give you an honest sense of cost and timing before we set off. When we arrive, we diagnose the fault properly and agree a fixed price with you before any repair work starts — you will never be presented with a bill you haven't already agreed to.

If the job turns out to need parts we don't carry on the van, or work beyond a straightforward emergency repair — for example a full boiler repair or a system fault — we'll explain that clearly and agree next steps with you rather than leaving you guessing.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Are you actually available out of hours?

Sometimes, but we won't pretend otherwise: we're a one-person operation, not a 24/7 call centre. We aim to respond as quickly as we can, and emergency call-outs are subject to availability at the time you call. If we're already on a job, we'll tell you honestly rather than leave you waiting without an answer.

Where is my stopcock likely to be in a Wirral property?

It varies by property type. Most commonly it's under the kitchen sink. In many Wirral homes it's near the front of the property, sometimes in an external meter box. In older terraced houses it can be tucked under the stairs. It's worth locating yours now, before an emergency, so you're not searching for it in a panic.

How much is an emergency call-out?

We don't quote a fixed figure over the phone, because emergency jobs vary too much in scale to make that honest. We'll give you a sense of cost when you call, and always agree a fixed price with you before any repair work starts — you'll never be charged for anything you haven't agreed to first.

I can smell gas — what should I do?

Leave the property immediately. Don't use light switches, phones, or any naked flames inside. Once you're safely outside, call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 straight away — not us. They're the correct people to make the situation safe, and they operate a genuine 24-hour emergency line.

Do you cover Liverpool as well as Wirral?

Yes, we cover parts of Merseyside including Liverpool, as well as the whole of Wirral. Give us a call and let us know your location — we'll tell you honestly whether we can get to you and roughly when.
