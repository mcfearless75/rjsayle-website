What's Included

What a Proper Service Includes

A genuine annual service is a technical inspection, not a quick wipe-down. On every boiler service we carry out, we check and record the following.

Combustion analysis — we test the flue gases with a calibrated analyser to confirm the boiler is burning gas cleanly and safely, not producing excess carbon monoxide.

Seal and gasket checks — casing seals, door gaskets and jointing are inspected for wear or damage that could let flue gases escape into the room.

Flue integrity check — the flue is inspected for correct termination, secure joints and no obstruction, so combustion products are safely vented outside.

Gas rate check — we confirm the boiler is burning gas at the correct rate for its rated output, which affects both safety and efficiency.

Working pressures — gas and water pressures are measured against the manufacturer's specification and adjusted where needed.

Condensate trap & pipe check — the condensate trap is checked and cleared and the pipe run is inspected, since a blocked trap is one of the most common causes of a boiler lockout.

Magnetic filter clean — if your system has a magnetic filter fitted, we open it up and clean out the sludge it's collected. This is the single biggest indicator of how your heating system is coping, and it's routinely skipped by services that are rushed.

The Difference

What a Bad Service Looks Like

A wipe-down and a sticker on the casing is not a service. If an engineer is at your boiler for ten minutes, doesn't connect a combustion analyser, doesn't open the case to check the seals, and doesn't ask when the filter was last cleaned, you haven't had a proper service — you've had a visit.

We won't name names, but the corners that get cut when a job is priced to be quick are predictable: no combustion analysis (or one done without the analyser actually being calibrated), no check of the condensate trap until it blocks and the boiler locks out in December, and the magnetic filter left untouched year after year until it's so full of black sludge it can barely do its job.

None of that shows up on the day. It shows up eighteen months later as a breakdown, a failed warranty claim, or a heating system that's noticeably less efficient than it used to be. A proper service costs more time on site because it actually is more work — that's the point of it.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

We don't quote a fixed price for a service without seeing the boiler, because a handful of things genuinely change how long the job takes and what's involved. The boiler type matters — a combi, system boiler or heat-only boiler each have a different service routine. Access matters too: a boiler in an open kitchen cupboard is a different job to one squeezed into a loft or airing cupboard with restricted access.

Age is another factor. An older boiler that's never had its filter or heat exchanger cleaned properly can take considerably longer to service than a newer one that's been looked after. If a magnetic filter or system needs a proper flush rather than a quick clean, that's additional work on top of the service itself.

Local Knowledge

Why Servicing Matters More in a Soft-Water Area — Sludge, Not Scale

A lot of boiler advice you'll find online is written with hard-water areas in mind, where limescale is the main enemy of a heating system. Wirral and Merseyside aren't like that. Our water is soft — around 73ppm compared with a UK average closer to 230ppm — so scale build-up inside pipework and heat exchangers isn't the problem it is further south or in the Midlands.

That doesn't mean local heating systems are trouble-free. The failure mode here is different: magnetite, better known as black sludge, forms as the steel in radiators and pipework corrodes over time and mixes with the system water. It settles in the bottom of radiators, clogs pumps, and — if you have one fitted — builds up in the magnetic filter.

A boiler that's noisy, slow to heat up, or has radiators that are cold at the bottom but warm at the top is usually telling you it's got a sludge problem, not a scale problem. Servicing catches this early. Opening and cleaning the magnetic filter every year, and flagging a system that's building up sludge faster than expected, is exactly the kind of thing a proper service is for — and exactly the kind of thing a rushed one misses.

Protect Your Cover

Servicing and Your Manufacturer Warranty

Most new boilers come with an extended manufacturer warranty — often well beyond the standard one or two years — but that extended cover usually comes with a condition attached: an annual service by a Gas Safe registered engineer, carried out and recorded on schedule, every single year.

Miss a year, and depending on the manufacturer, you can find the warranty reduced or invalidated altogether — right when a costly part failure is the reason you'd want to make a claim. We won't tell you every manufacturer's exact terms here, because they vary and change, but the general rule holds across the industry: keep your service record complete, keep the receipts, and don't let a year slip.

We provide a service record and invoice for every visit, so you've always got the paperwork to back up a warranty claim if you ever need one.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a boiler service take?

A proper service normally takes around 45 minutes to an hour on a straightforward, accessible boiler. It can take longer on an older boiler, one that's hard to access, or one that hasn't been serviced in a while and needs a thorough clean of the main heat exchanger or filter. If an engineer is in and out in ten minutes, that's not a full service.

Does skipping a service void my warranty?

It can. Many manufacturers make an annual service by a Gas Safe registered engineer a condition of keeping an extended parts and labour warranty valid. Miss a year and you risk invalidating cover you've already paid for, right when you might need it most. Check your specific boiler's warranty terms, but as a general rule — don't assume a gap is fine.

Is a service the same as a gas safety certificate?

No, they're different jobs. A gas safety certificate (CP12) is a legal safety check confirming your gas appliances are safe to use — it's a landlord requirement for rental properties. A boiler service is a more detailed technical inspection and maintenance visit aimed at keeping the boiler running efficiently and protecting your warranty. We can carry out both in the same visit. Read more on our gas safety certificate page.

What's the best time of year to book?

Late summer to early autumn, before the weather turns. Book in August or September and you'll get a convenient appointment before demand spikes. Leave it until the first cold snap in October or November and you're competing with everyone else whose boiler has just failed.

My boiler seems fine — do I really need it serviced?

Yes. A boiler running fine today can still be building up sludge, drifting out of its correct gas rate, or developing a slow flue leak — none of which you'd notice day to day. Servicing catches these issues early, before they turn into a breakdown or a safety issue, and keeps your warranty intact.
