---

### Verified policy positions this plan is built on

Four things changed or are widely misreported. They shape the plan, so they're stated up front:

1. **GBP messaging/chat is dead.** Google discontinued chat and call history on **31 July 2024**. Any guide telling you to "turn on messaging and reply within 24 hours" is out of date. Section 11 is shorter than you'd expect because of this.
2. **Radius service areas no longer exist.** Google's current guidance is explicit: *"You can't set your service area as a radius distance around your business."* Named areas only — max **20**, and the overall boundary should be **no more than ~2 hours' drive** from base.
3. **Review content direction was banned on 17 April 2026.** Google's Rating Manipulation policy now prohibits *"merchants requesting that staff solicit reviews that include specific content, including content that identifies a staff member."* The permitted-conduct clause allows soliciting genuine reviews only *"without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."* **This kills the standard "ask them to mention 'boiler installation Heswall'" tactic.** Section 9 is rewritten around it.
4. **Photo geotagging does nothing.** Google strips EXIF on upload. It is a myth. Detail in section 6.

## 1. Categories

### Final call

| Slot | Category |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Plumber** |
| Secondary 1 | Heating contractor |
| Secondary 2 | Gas installation service |
| Secondary 3 | Boiler supplier |
| Secondary 4 | Hot water system supplier |

**Why "Plumber" primary, not "Heating contractor".** This is the one genuinely contested call, because his margin is in boiler installs (£2–4k) not in tap washers. Three reasons Plumber still wins:

- **Query surface.** "Plumber" is the UK vernacular head term and Google maps an unusually wide range of plumbing *and* heating queries onto it. "Heating contractor" is a US-derived label with thin UK colloquial usage — nobody on the Wirral searches "heating contractor near me."
- **You don't forfeit boiler queries.** Secondary categories confer local pack eligibility. Heating contractor + Boiler supplier as secondaries keep him eligible for "new boiler Wirral" and "boiler installation Bebington"; he loses a marginal relevance edge, not the entitlement.
- **Cash flow for a one-man band.** Reactive plumbing volume funds the diary between installs. Primary category is the single biggest lever on which pack he appears in at all.

**Documented switch test.** Review at day 90. If the enquiry mix is >70% sub-£150 reactive work and install enquiries are flat, switch primary to **Heating contractor** and demote Plumber to secondary. Change primary category *once* and leave it 90 days — flip-flopping resets the profile's trust signals and can trigger re-review.

### Categories to AVOID, and why

- **HVAC contractor** — US term, and it drags him into air-conditioning packs. Air source heat pumps are *not* air conditioning. Wrong leads, and a mismatch between category and what the site/photos evidence is a suspension vector.
- **Air conditioning contractor** — same problem, worse. He doesn't do F-Gas aircon. Actively avoid.
- **Contractor / Repair service** — generic junk categories with no meaningful pack surface. Pure dilution, zero upside.
- **Bathroom remodeler** — only add if he genuinely fits full bathrooms. It isn't in the stated service list. Adding it invites bathroom-fit enquiries he'll turn down, which suppresses conversion signals.
- **Drainage service** — *tempting but decline.* He does above-ground drainage; UK "drainage" search intent is overwhelmingly blocked drains and jetting, which he doesn't do. Lead mismatch.
- **Mechanical contractor** — the only defensible sixth. Relevant to commercial fit-outs, but commercial M&E work is won on relationships and tenders, not the Wirral map pack. Skip for now; revisit only if commercial becomes the strategic focus.

**On category count:** BrightLocal's study found rankings improved with additional categories up to around four, with no evidence of dilution — but only for genuinely relevant ones. Five total is the right number here. Don't pad to nine.

## 2. Service-area business setup

**Address handling.** Enter the home address during signup — Google requires it for verification — then **remove it from display**. Google's rule is unambiguous: a service-area business with no customer-facing premises must not show an address at all. Leaving a home address visible on a van-based business is both a privacy problem and, in some categories, a suspension trigger. Once hidden, the profile shows only the service areas.

**Verification.** Expect **video verification**. Have ready in one continuous unedited take: the van with signage and reg, branded workwear, the Gas Safe ID card, tools/stock, then walk to the business paperwork (Certificate of Incorporation, insurance schedule) and finally show yourself. Do not stop recording mid-way — restarts are the most common rejection cause. Mobile-only phone number is fine; Google does not require a landline.

**Radius vs named areas.** Radius is not available. Named areas only.

**How many areas — recommendation: 8, not 20.**

```
Birkenhead · Wallasey · Bebington · Heswall
West Kirby · Hoylake · Neston · Ellesmere Port
```

Deliberately **excluded: Liverpool and Chester.** Two reasons. First, service areas are a *filtering* signal, not a ranking one — listing a town does not make you rank in it. Ranking in Liverpool is driven by proximity to the searcher, and a Wirral-pinned business will not out-rank Liverpool-pinned plumbers in Liverpool packs regardless of what's in the box. Second, a service area sprawling across the Mersey dilutes the profile's perceived centroid and is the classic over-reach pattern that correlates with quality-review flags.

**Risk to flag:** the site says *"happy to travel anywhere in the UK for the right job."* That's fine as website copy, but do **not** attempt to reflect nationwide coverage in the GBP service areas — it breaches the ~2-hour drive guidance and is a genuine suspension risk. Nationwide commercial capability belongs in the business description and on the site, not in the service area field.

## 6. Photos

### Geotagging — the plain answer

**It does not work. It has never worked. Google strips EXIF metadata on upload.** Every "geotag your photos to rank in more suburbs" tool and blog post is selling a placebo. Do not pay for a geotagging service, do not install an EXIF editor, do not spend a minute on it. This is the single most common piece of wasted effort in local SEO for trades.

What *does* carry signal: **upload from a phone that has been at the job**, uploaded through the Google Maps app while logged into the business account. Location association there comes from the account/device session, not the file. Even that is a weak signal — treat it as free rather than as a strategy.

### What to actually shoot

| Type | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Before/after boiler installs** | 20+ pairs | Highest-converting asset by a distance. The old back boiler → new combi pair sells the job. |
| **Van + signage** | 3–5 | Verification evidence and legitimacy. |
| **Engineer at work, face visible** | 10+ | Trust. A one-man band's face *is* the brand. |
| **Unvented cylinder installs** | 8+ | Differentiates from general plumbers. High-ticket. |
| **Neat pipework / commissioning** | 10+ | Craft evidence. Trades buyers judge on tidiness. |
| **Gas Safe card / certificates** | 2–3 | Credential proof. |
| **Commercial fit-outs** | 10+ | Only assets that support commercial enquiries. |
| **Logo / cover** | 2 | Cover photo: van with branding. Logo: clean square. |

**Cadence:** upload **5–8 photos per week**, ongoing, not a 200-photo dump on day one. A steady drip is a freshness/activity signal; a single bulk upload followed by silence is not. Realistically: shoot every job on the phone, upload Friday afternoon, 10 minutes.

**Quality rules:** landscape, well-lit, no filters, no text overlays or watermarks (Google may reject or demote them), minimum 720px. **Never upload a photo containing a customer's house number, door, or car registration.**

**Low-ROI, skip:** 360° virtual tours (nothing to tour), stock photography (actively harmful — customers spot it), video posts (production cost outweighs return for a sole trader).

## 9. Review engineering

### Read this before writing any review request

The standard trade-SEO advice — *"ask the customer to mention the service and the town in their review"* — became a **policy violation on 17 April 2026**. Google's Rating Manipulation policy now prohibits soliciting reviews containing specific content, and the permitted-conduct clause allows soliciting genuine reviews only *"without... attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."*

You asked for keyword and area-name prompts. **My recommendation is not to use them.** Enforcement is pattern-based — Google looks for unusual volume, timing clusters and device correlation — so a sole trader is unlikely to be caught on any single request. But the failure mode is asymmetric: a filtered review batch or a profile flag costs far more than the marginal ranking gain from a few keyword-stuffed reviews. And a wall of reviews that all say "great boiler installation in Heswall" reads as fake to human buyers, which is the audience that actually converts.

**The compliant lever that achieves the same outcome: your replies.** Owner responses are your copy, on your profile, with no content restriction. Reply to every review and put the service term and the place name in *your* reply. That gets the keyword association without touching the customer's contribution.

Example — customer writes *"Brilliant job, really tidy, highly recommend."* You reply:

> Thanks very much, Sarah. Glad you're happy with the new combi boiler installation in West Kirby — it was a straightforward swap in the end and the system's running nicely. Any issues with the controls over the next few weeks just give me a ring. — Rob

### UK incentive rules — plainly

**Google:** no payment, discounts, free goods or services in exchange for a review, a revised review, or removal of a negative one. No exceptions, no "we'll enter you into a prize draw."

**UK law is stricter than Google.** Under the **Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024**, which brought fake and incentivised reviews into scope as a banned practice enforced directly by the CMA, publishing incentivised reviews without clearly disclosing the incentive is unlawful — and the CMA can fine up to **10% of global turnover** without going to court first. **Do not incentivise reviews.** Not a discount, not a prize draw, not a free service. There is no clever workaround here.

**Also banned:** review gating — screening customers by expected sentiment and only asking the happy ones. Ask everyone or ask no-one.

### The system for a one-man band

The failure mode is always the same: he means to ask, the day runs away, he's on the next job. Fix it with a **fixed trigger point**, not willpower.

**The trigger: ask at the moment of handover, in person, before leaving.** Highest conversion point by far. Then send the link within 30 minutes while he's still in the van outside.

**Two-step, and stop there:**

**Step 1 — verbal, at handover**
> "One last thing before I head off — if you've been happy with the job, a quick Google review makes a genuine difference to a one-man business like mine. I'll text you the link now, takes about thirty seconds. No pressure either way."

**Step 2 — SMS, sent from the van**
> Hi [Name], Rob from R.J. Sayle Plumbing & Heating. Thanks again for having me out today. If you've got a spare minute, a Google review would really help me out: [short link]. Any problems with anything, ring me first and I'll come back out. Cheers, Rob

**Step 3 — one follow-up only, 4 days later, then never again**
> Hi [Name], Rob here — just a gentle nudge on that Google review if you've got a moment: [short link]. No worries at all if not. Cheers.

**Email version** (for landlords and commercial clients, who respond better to email):

> Subject: Thanks from R.J. Sayle Plumbing & Heating
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> Thanks for having us out to [property/site] — I hope everything's running as it should.
>
> If you were happy with how the job went, I'd be grateful if you'd leave us a Google review. It's the main way people find a small business like ours, and it takes less than a minute:
>
> [review link]
>
> If anything isn't right, please reply to this email or ring me on 07450 237593 before leaving feedback and I'll get it sorted.
>
> Many thanks,
> Rob Sayle
> R.J. Sayle Plumbing & Heating Ltd — Gas Safe registered

Note that last line does double duty: it's a genuine service offer, and it routes dissatisfaction to the phone rather than the profile — which is legitimate, because it's offered to *everyone*, not selectively. That distinction is what keeps it the right side of gating.

**Mechanics:**
- Get the short review link from GBP ("Ask for reviews"). Save it as a **phone text-replacement shortcut** (type `revlink`, expands to the URL). Removes all friction.
- **Target: 4 asks a week → realistically 1–2 reviews a week → 50–80/year.** That is category-dominant for a Wirral plumber.
- **Pace it.** 30 reviews in a fortnight after months of nothing is the exact velocity spike that triggers filtering. Steady beats bursty.
- **Reply to every single review within 48 hours**, positive and negative. Replies are visible, they're a live activity signal, and they're your only compliant keyword surface.

**Fix first:** the site's schema claims **27 reviews in one place and 15 in another**, and neither is verified. Mismatched review-count structured data is both a Google structured-data spam issue and, if the numbers overstate reality, a DMCCA misleading-practice risk. Get the true count from GBP and make the site match it — or strip the `aggregateRating` markup entirely until it can be maintained accurately. Do this in week 1.

## 10. Products

**Recommendation: skip it. Low ROI.**

The Products section exists and is technically available, but for a service-area plumber it's the wrong tool — it's built for retail SKUs with prices and buy paths, and Google renders it as a shopping-style carousel. A plumber has no SKUs. Every job is quoted on survey.

The argument *for* is that product entries can be surfaced in AI-generated Maps summaries. That's real but marginal, and the same information is already carried properly by the **Services** section, which is the correct field for this business type and which section 3 populates thoroughly.

**If he insists on using it** — say, downtime in January — the only defensible use is 4–6 *boiler range* entries (e.g. "Worcester Bosch Greenstar Combi Boiler", "Vaillant ecoTEC Plus", "Ideal Logic Combi") with no price, or "Price on request", linking to the relevant site page. Cap it at 30 minutes total. **Do not** list "Boiler Installation" as a product — that's a service, it belongs in Services, and duplicating it adds nothing.

**Verdict: busywork. Do Services properly instead.**

## 11. Booking and messaging

**Messaging: unavailable. Nothing to set up.** Google discontinued Business Profile chat, chat FAQs, quote requests and call history on **31 July 2024**. The `BUSINESS_CONVERSATIONS` metric was removed from the Performance API at the same time. If any tool or agency proposes "GBP messaging setup" as a line item, it's a fabricated deliverable.

**Booking button: almost certainly unavailable, don't chase it.** The Book button is populated through Reserve with Google via integrated scheduling partners. Coverage in the UK is concentrated in hospitality, beauty and fitness. There is no realistic trade-plumbing integration path for a sole trader, and building one is wildly disproportionate.

**What to do instead — the call is the conversion event.**

1. **Verify the phone number is exactly right and answered.** 07450 237593, mobile only, and that is fine — Google does not require a landline. But the entire profile funnels to one number on one handset held by a man who is frequently on his back under a boiler.
2. **Fix the missed-call problem — this is the single highest-ROI item in this document.** A Wirral plumber ranking well will take 15–30 calls a week. Every unanswered call is a customer who dials the next result within 20 seconds. Set up an **auto-SMS on missed call** ("Sorry I missed you — I'm on a job. Text me what you need and I'll ring back within the hour. Rob"). Costs a few pounds a month. It will recover more revenue than every other item on this list combined.
3. **Website button:** point it at `https://rjsayleplumbing-heating.com` with UTM tagging (see section 12).
4. **Set hours accurately and use Special Hours** for bank holidays and Christmas. Wrong hours on a bank holiday means "Closed" on the profile while he's actually working.

**Fix the hours inconsistency in week 1.** The site shows Mon–Fri 07:30–18:00 in one place and 08:00–18:00 plus Sat 08:00–13:00 in another. Pick one, set it on GBP, and make every site page match. Emergency callouts should be described in the business description and services — **do not set 24/7 hours** unless he genuinely answers the phone at 3am, because "Open now" filtering will send him 3am calls and generate one-star reviews when he doesn't pick up.

## 12. Measurement

**What actually matters** (GBP Performance tab, 6-month rolling window):

| Metric | Why | Realistic direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Calls** | The conversion event. The only number that maps to revenue. | Primary KPI |
| **Website clicks** | Secondary intent | Supporting |
| **Direction requests** | *Low value for a SAB* — nobody drives to a van. Near-zero. Ignore. | Ignore |
| **Searches breakdown** (queries) | Shows which terms surface the profile — the closest thing to keyword data GBP gives | Highest diagnostic value |
| **Discovery vs Direct vs Branded** | Discovery growth = new demand capture. Branded growth = reputation. | Watch the Discovery share |
| **Views (Search vs Maps)** | Vanity, mostly. Don't report on it. | Context only |

**Two caveats before anyone builds a dashboard:** GBP's Performance data lags roughly 2–5 days, and "Views" methodology has been changed by Google more than once, creating step-changes that look like performance shifts but aren't. Never report a month-on-month view delta without checking for a methodology change.

**Set up properly in week 1:**
- **UTM-tag the website link:** `?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp` — otherwise GBP traffic is indistinguishable from organic in analytics.
- **Export Performance data monthly.** GBP only retains 6 months. If it isn't exported it's gone. A single Google Sheet, appended monthly.
- **Call tracking:** a dynamic-number-insertion tracking number in GBP is the classic NAP-consistency risk. If tracking is wanted, the safe implementation is the tracking number as the **primary** and the real mobile as an **additional** number — but honestly, for a sole trader, **skip it**. Ask customers "how did you find me?" and log it. Simpler, free, good enough.

### Monthly review checklist (30 minutes)

1. Export Performance data; log calls, website clicks, Discovery share.
2. Review the search queries report — note new terms; add any that reveal a genuine service gap to the Services list.
3. Check review count and average; confirm every review has a reply.
4. Check for **unauthorised profile edits** — Google auto-applies edits from users and third-party data. Categories, hours and the website URL get changed silently. **This is the most common cause of an inexplicable ranking drop.**
5. Confirm the address is still hidden and service areas are intact.
6. Confirm photos uploaded that month (target 20–30).
7. Confirm 4 posts published.
8. Check Q&A for new public questions.
9. Run the geo-grid scan (section 13); compare to last month.
10. Check hours against upcoming bank holidays; set Special Hours.

## 13. Geo-grid ranking

**Tools.** **Local Falcon** is the category standard and the right pick — per-scan credit pricing suits a single location, and the share-of-local-voice metric is the one worth tracking over time. **BrightLocal** bundles grid tracking into a broader local SEO suite and is better value if citation and audit tooling is also wanted. **Places Scout** is agency-grade and overkill here. For a single profile, budget roughly **£20–30/month**; anything more is over-tooled.

**Scan configuration:** 7×7 grid, 1-mile spacing, centred on the Wirral base. That covers the peninsula properly without wasting credits on the Irish Sea and the Dee estuary. Track 5 terms, no more:

```
plumber near me
boiler installation
new boiler
emergency plumber
boiler repair
```

Run **monthly**, same day each month, same time of day. Weekly scanning is credit-burn for no added insight — local rankings don't move meaningfully week to week.

### What to realistically expect

Be blunt with the client about this, because grid maps are seductive and generate unrealistic expectations.

**Proximity dominates everything in the local pack.** For "plumber near me" the searcher's own location is the strongest single input. A Wirral-based sole trader will show **green in the 2–4 miles around base and red beyond it**, and no amount of optimisation changes that fundamentally. This is not a failure — it is how the algorithm works. Anyone promising an all-green grid across the whole peninsula is selling something.

**Where gains are genuinely achievable:**
- **Radius extension** from ~2 miles to ~4–5 miles around base — realistic and worth pursuing.
- **Higher-intent, lower-volume terms** — "unvented cylinder installation", "power flushing", "landlord gas safety certificate". Thin competition on the Wirral. Expect top-3 across a much wider area on these than on "plumber near me", and note that these are also the **higher-margin** jobs. Track at least two of them.
- **Branded and semi-branded** — near-total dominance, quickly.

**Timeline:**

| Period | Expected |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** | Nothing. Baseline scan only. Resist the urge to read anything into it. |
| **Months 2–3** | First movement. Category and services changes settle. Grid tightens around base. |
| **Months 3–6** | The real curve — driven almost entirely by **review velocity**, not by profile fields. Meaningful radius growth if reviews are landing weekly. |
| **Months 6–12** | Consolidation. Top-3 across most of the Wirral for the specialist terms; competitive-but-proximity-bound on head terms. |

**The honest summary:** the profile optimisation in sections 1–8 is a one-off job worth maybe 30% of the outcome. **Reviews and answered calls are the other 70%,** and they're ongoing. If only one thing gets done, do section 9.
