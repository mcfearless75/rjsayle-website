## 3. Service × Area matrix — the decision

### The call: build **8** service×area pages. Not 165. Not 33. Eight.

The maximum theoretical matrix here is 15 services × 11 areas = **165 pages**. homeheatingwirral runs roughly 37 areas × 2 service types ≈ 74. You should not copy them.

**Why 8, and the arithmetic behind it.** Wirral's population is ~320,000. Total genuine monthly search volume for *all* plumbing/heating queries across the peninsula is modest — meaningful "[service] [town]" volume exists for maybe three services (new boiler, boiler repair, emergency plumber) in maybe five towns. Beyond that you are creating pages for keywords that get single-digit or zero searches, at real cost: crawl dilution, internal-link equity spread thin across near-duplicates, and an unmistakable doorway-page signal across a site that already has 11 templated area pages.

You are one person. Every page must earn its keep in *hours of your time*, and the marginal page beyond ~8 cannot.

### The doorway-page line — where this tips over, and how to stay on the right side

Google's doorway policy targets pages "created to funnel visitors into the actual usable or relevant portion of your site" and clusters of pages "similar to each other, with the only difference being the location or city name." **Your 11 existing area pages currently sit on the wrong side of that line** — ~70% identical body, plus three FAQs that differ only by a town name. That's the textbook example. This is the risk that matters, more than any new page you might add.

**Hard gate. Do not publish a service×area page unless all five are true:**

1. **60%+ of the body is unique** — not spun, not synonym-swapped. Genuinely different sentences about a genuinely different place.
2. **It contains at least one real, completed job in that town**, with the street or district named, the boiler make and model, the actual problem, and a photo. If you haven't done a job there, you don't get the page yet.
3. **It names specific local detail a non-local could not write** — housing stock era, a named conservation area, postcode districts, a landmark, a known system quirk.
4. **The FAQs are different questions**, not the same question with a different noun. "Do you cover Heswall?" appearing on eleven pages is the signal that gets a cluster classified as doorways.
5. **It converts or it goes.** Review at 6 months. Zero organic entrances or zero calls → merge it into the parent service page with a 301 and move on.

**Additional safeguards:**
- Never generate these programmatically. If you find yourself in a spreadsheet with a `{{town}}` variable, stop.
- Cap at 8. Adding "just a few more" is exactly how a legitimate local cluster becomes a doorway cluster.
- Each page must link *up* to the parent service page and *sideways* to the area page — a genuine hierarchy, not a flat mat of terminal pages.

## Blunt summary: what NOT to build

1. **Do not build a service × area matrix.** 165 pages, or even 33, from a one-man Wirral operation is a doorway-page cluster with extra steps. **Eight**, each gated on a real local job. That's the number.
2. **Do not add new geo pages before fixing the 11 you have.** They're currently ~70% templated with three identical town-swapped FAQs. That is the textbook doorway pattern, and it is your largest existing risk. §4 is a prerequisite, not a parallel workstream.
3. **Do not build a finance page** without an FCA-regulated finance partner actually in place.
4. **Do not build six boiler brand pages.** Build one comparison page, later, if at all. They attract people shopping the boiler, not the installer.
5. **Do not build an air conditioning page.** You don't offer it.
6. **Do not build heat pump pages if you are not MCS certified.** BUS grants legally require an MCS-certified installer. Publishing grant content you can't fulfil generates unqualified leads, wastes your evenings, and damages trust. Get MCS, or leave the £7,500/£9,000 market alone and say so honestly.
7. **Do not claim 24/7 or 60-minute response.** You are one person. Overclaiming availability is the fastest route to one-star reviews, and Google prices reviews into local rankings.
8. **Do not write generic national blog content.** "10 signs you need a new boiler" is worthless — a hundred sites outrank you on it already. Every post must be locally, policy-, or data-anchored.
9. **Do not fabricate reviews or review schema.** Manual-action risk, and unnecessary — you have 20+ years of real customers.
10. **Do not leave 24 photos hotlinked from Wix.** They can vanish at any moment, they're invisible to image search, and they're currently the single most wasted asset on the site.

**The three things that matter most, in order:** (1) self-host the images and publish the Gas Safe number — this week; (2) de-templatise the 11 area pages before doing anything else geographic; (3) start photographing and writing up every job. Item 3 compounds indefinitely and no competitor with a marketing agency can replicate it, because it requires actually being on site.
